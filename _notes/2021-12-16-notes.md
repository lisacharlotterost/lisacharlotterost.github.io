It's the last working day of the year for me. I emailed another ten people who had interesting questions in the survey if they'd like to answer some questions in a call in January (three people already replied and scheduled calls 🎉). 

I also wrote to [**Jonatan Hildén**](https://twitter.com/jhilden). He's one of the two authors of the excellent [**Data Visualization Handbook**](https://datavizhandbook.info/), the maybe most beautiful collection of how to's in data vis there is. He also filled out the survey and made a great comment on accessibility of colors in it. So I got in touch with him today, and he immediately replied and made me aware of a [series of articles](https://observablehq.com/@frankelavsky/chartability-contrast-series) on the concept of "high contrast" in data vis by Frank Elavsky. 

![border](/pic/notes/Screenshot_2021-12-16_at_17.42.12.png)

from ["Experimenting with 'No Use of Color Alone' in Data Visualizations with Sequential, Diverging, and Circular Color Scales"](https://observablehq.com/@frankelavsky/experimental-color-scale-textures) by Frank Elavsky

Now, Frank is a known figure in the world of what he calls "[chartability](https://chartability.fizz.studio/)". If you research "accessibility in data vis" for two hours, you'll stumble upon Frank. That said, I didn't know his article series, and just read through them. It's fascinating to see his articles, and to see the implications of what happens when you apply the WCAG 2 to data vis. 

Jonathan also pointed me towards the paper ["Modeling Color Difference for Visualization Design"](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/VisColors/) by [Danielle Albers Szafir](https://danielleszafir.com/); and I discovered [this contrast introduction](https://webaim.org/articles/contrast/), and [this little tool](https://bl.ocks.org/connorgr/84aaa3a86b7c1231be5221d26591a906) by [Connor Gramazio](https://gramaz.io/). I haven't quite understood it yet, but I will. In January. After some nice vacations.
