I love putting stuff online. Since I was 16 (that's 20 years ago), I've been doing so – on now-dead forums, now-dead social networks, and surprisingly still-alive blogs. Seeing the things I created online makes them better, I find. I've shared [photos](/notes/2006/12/22) I took from my high school at 16, [work](/notes/2008/01/08) with which I applied for art school at 18, and enjoyed documenting my [master's thesis](/everything#masters-thesis) (2014) and a [fellowship](/everything#fellowship-in-dc) (2016) almost daily. And over the last 16 years, I've learned to love and then mourn [Twitter](/everything#tweet).

For these ~1,100 posts from my past, I created an archive called ["Everything."](/everything) 

![](/pic/260309_everything-screenshot.png)

It includes:

- 493 [tweets](/everything#tweet) that I posted first as @lisacrost (2009–2021), then as @lisacmuth 
- 204 photos from an old Blogger blog from when I was 16–18 years old
- 170 [drawings](/everything#drawings) from an old Blogger blog from when I was 17–19 years old
- 25 posts [documenting](/everything#new-york-city) an [internship at Bloomberg](/bloomberg) in New York City in 2013, originally on Tumblr
- 99 posts [documenting](/everything#masters-thesis) designing my [Master’s Thesis](/mastersthesis) in 2014 (Tumblr)
- 133 posts [documenting](https://lisacharlottemuth.com/everything#fellowship-in-dc) my OpenNews fellowship in 2016 (Tumblr)
- 131 posts [documenting](https://datawrapper.notion.site/Color-Book-Updates-54905c2bd0bb4c6bae15d99e31a9d5c4) my research for the book on colors I’m writing (>2021), originally on Notion
- 14 (so far) book reviews from [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/user/show/76557418-lisa-muth)
- 12 thoughts about data vis books I read for the [Data Vis Book Club](/datavisbookclub), from the written discussions about those books. 

I curated. Many of my 5,100+ tweets are boring (“Don’t miss our [data vis meetup](/everything#data-vis-meetup-berlin) tomorrow!”), so I only kept the ones I want to preserve for the future. You won’t find replies or pure retweets in there, either. The same goes for my drawings and the photos I took as a teenager: I hope I hit a sweet spot between showing authentically how 17-year-old Lisa saw the world and showing stuff that’s still interesting somehow.

## [Notes](/notes): My little corner of the internet

On "Everything," you'll also find the 30 posts that I shared over the past three months in another new section of my website: "Notes." This is where you can watch me share sometimes boring, always unpolished thoughts. I upload [photos](/notes/2026/03/01) I took that day or the day before, [doodles](/notes/2025/12/06), [book reviews](/everything#books), visual experiments, and work in progress. ([Here's a full article on how I'm using RSS to post to this Jekyll blog on my phone](/rss-jekyll-blog), if you're interested in that.)


![](/pic/260309_notes-screenshot.png)

What I’m doing there is different from what I usually do. When I write on the [Datawrapper blog](https://blog.datawrapper.de/) and on social media, I think a lot about you: what you’ll find interesting or useful or fun. I research, structure and restructure, edit and proofread — all so that you get to read something that’s as valuable as I can create. I enjoy doing so, and it makes me really happy to know that you enjoy reading it. 

“Notes,” however, is my little corner of the internet. It’s a small, cozy room of my own where I go if I don’t want to think about you for a while. Here, I write for myself. I still censor myself — this is the internet, after all, not my actual journal or best friend — but I post lots of things that wouldn’t feel right as a proper article, on the Datawrapper blog, or on social media. 

That said: I won’t mind guests! Do visit my little room, or at least peek through the window from time to time. In fact, I created an [RSS feed](/everything.xml) for you to do just that.


## ROOTS before POSSE

Why am I doing all this? Because I got inspired by the concept of [**POSSE**](https://indieweb.org/POSSE): "Publish on your own, syndicate elsewhere." For me, **ROOTS** is the logical first step toward that: "Return Old Online Things to your own Site" (yes, I made this up). Why? If I do decide to delete my X account or if Blogger gets quietly discontinued, then I don't care: it's all on my site already. I own it. It's all Markdown files and images that I can back up anywhere I want.  

You’ll see me POSSE (or [PESOS](https://indieweb.org/PESOS) – “Publish Elsewhere, Syndicate (to your) Own Site”) in the future, too: If I post a Goodreads review, it’ll also be on my Notes and Everything pages. If I post on LinkedIn, it’ll be there, too. Everything I create and find important will eventually end up on my website. 

## Bonus: No more cookies for you!

If you’ve been on my website before, you might notice that something else has changed: I don’t have a cookie warning anymore. I deleted my Google Analytics tracking code. And I only [show you those old Disqus comments on articles](/notes/2026/01/27) where they existed, and only if you agree to it. So: I don’t know if two people read my notes (my mom and I) or hundreds. I like that.
